# Lab 03: Exploring Neural Information Processing

## Overview
This lab explored how biological neurons and artificial neurons process information. The assignment focused on comparing action potentials in biological systems with activation functions used in artificial neural networks. Through simulations and experiments, I examined how neurons integrate input, generate output signals, and form simple networks capable of processing information.

## What I Did
- Implemented a simplified biological neuron model in Python using membrane potential, threshold behavior, and reset dynamics.
- Simulated action potentials and observed how varying input current affected neuron firing behavior.
- Built an artificial neuron model using weighted inputs and activation functions such as sigmoid, ReLU, and tanh.
- Compared biological neuron behavior with artificial neuron processing.
- Created simple neural network structures to observe how information flows through connected neurons.
- Generated plots and visualizations showing membrane potential changes and activation outputs.
- Conducted experiments analyzing how network architecture and activation functions influence system behavior.

## What I Learned
This lab helped me better understand the similarities and differences between biological and artificial neural systems. I learned that biological neurons rely on dynamic electrical processes such as threshold-triggered spikes and refractory periods, while artificial neurons simplify processing into mathematical activation functions. I also observed how network structure influences information flow and how concepts from neuroscience inspire modern AI architectures.

Additionally, I gained a stronger understanding of how temporal behavior, signal integration, and nonlinear activation contribute to intelligent processing in both biological brains and artificial neural networks.

## Challenges
One of the most challenging parts of this lab was understanding how membrane potential changes over time in the biological neuron simulation. I also had to troubleshoot issues related to spike generation and threshold crossing in the code. Another challenge was interpreting how different activation functions affect artificial neuron outputs and comparing those behaviors to biological systems in a meaningful way.
